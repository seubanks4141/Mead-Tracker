01

Using a hydrometer

A hydrometer estimates the density of your must or mead compared with water. A clean reading gives you a useful way to follow fermentation.

  1. 1
    Take a representative sample

    Use sanitized equipment and enough liquid for the hydrometer to float without touching the bottom.

  2. 2
    Release bubbles

    Lower the hydrometer gently and give it a small spin so clinging bubbles do not lift it.

  3. 3
    Read at eye level

    Once it settles, read the scale at the bottom of the liquid’s curved surface, unless your instrument says otherwise.

  4. 4
    Record time and temperature

    Log the displayed SG, when you measured it, and the sample temperature if known.

02

What specific gravity means

Specific gravity (SG) compares a liquid’s density with water. Dissolved sugars usually make the starting must read above 1.000. As yeast consumes sugar, readings generally decrease.

03

OG, routine, and FG readings

Original gravity (OG)
The starting measurement, normally taken after ingredients are thoroughly mixed and before active fermentation changes the density.
Routine reading
Any measurement used to watch the fermentation trend. Several similar readings can help show that gravity has stabilized.
Final gravity (FG)
The reading you designate as final for recordkeeping and estimated ABV. “Final” should reflect your process, not a prediction from the software.
04

Sample temperature

Hydrometers are calibrated for a stated temperature, often printed on the instrument or its instructions. A sample far from that temperature can shift the reading. Record the actual sample temperature so you can apply the appropriate correction later.

△

Use the correction information supplied for your hydrometer. Mead Tracker stores your measurement; it does not assume every instrument has the same calibration.

05

Estimated alcohol by volume

Mead Tracker can estimate alcohol by volume from the readings marked original and final. It is an approximation, not a laboratory measurement, and is only as reliable as the entered readings.

( OG − FG ) × 131.25 ≈ ABV %

Recipes involving later sugar, honey, or fruit additions may require a more complete calculation than a simple OG/FG estimate.

06

Ingredients and units

Record ingredients as separate additions at the time they were used. This preserves staggered nutrients, secondary fruit, backsweetening honey, and spice adjustments in the correct order.

  • ✓Enter the number shown by the measuring tool.
  • ✓Choose a unit of the correct kind: weight, volume, or count.
  • ✓Use notes for preparation details such as frozen, toasted, split, or rehydrated.
  • ✓Create another addition when an ingredient goes in at a later time.
07

Sanitation reminders

This tracker helps with records, but it cannot verify that a process is safe. Clean and sanitize equipment that contacts the mead, follow the product instructions for your sanitizer, and avoid returning a test sample to the fermenter.

i

If a batch’s appearance, smell, pressure, or behavior raises a safety concern, seek experienced, qualified guidance rather than relying on software alone.

Labels and QR codes

The QR code is a shortcut to the batch’s phone-friendly update page. Before labeling bottles or a vessel:

For Avery Presta® 94051 stock, choose that preset to create an 18-position US Letter sheet of 2½ × 1½ inch oval labels. Choose any design from A through F; E, Contemporary Honeycomb, is the default. The selected design and border color appear in both the preview and PDF. Copies fill left to right and top to bottom. To reuse a partial sheet, set “Start at label” to the first unused position; for example, after printing four labels, start the next PDF at position 5.

  1. Make sure the configured site address is reachable from your phone and is not localhost.
  2. Print one test at “Actual size” or 100% scaling.
  3. Scan the physical print in ordinary lighting.
  4. Sign in and submit a harmless test observation, then remove it if desired.
09

Reading a batch visual mark

Each batch gets a small emblem that makes it easier to recognize at a glance. The center is its stable recipe identity; the outer treatment summarizes the currently recorded or estimated production stage. It is not a quality, readiness, or safety score.

Stable center
The center shape and colors identify this batch. Colors draw from active ingredient categories such as honey, fruit, water, spices, and nutrients.
Small dots
The dots are a compact reminder of ingredient categories represented in the current recipe record.
Identity, not stage
The center remains recognizable while the outside changes. A stage update does not turn the batch into a different visual identity.
Outer ring
Pattern, openness, and badges communicate how the current stage was recorded or estimated. A ring is never a universal aging clock.
  • ?

    Unknown. A dotted ring and question mark mean there is not enough usable information for a numeric estimate.

  • Fermenting, gravity based. A solid arc uses recorded original, latest, and target gravity values. It is an estimate and does not prove fermentation is finished.

  • ≈

    Optional time window. When enough recent readings form a useful trend, the gravity-based view may add a broad timing forecast beside the solid progress ring. Otherwise no time forecast is shown.

  • ◔

    Conditioning plan. A segmented treatment can show progress through a conditioning window you entered. That plan is not a chemical measurement.

  • Aging. There is no outer progress ring. The stable center remains, accompanied by plain elapsed-stage text on the batch record. There is no percentage and no universal finish line.

  • ✓

    Bottled or complete. A seal or check reflects the status you explicitly recorded; it is not calculated from elapsed time.

↔

One identity in two places. The same generated icon appears on the dashboard card and at the top of the batch detail page.

✓

Trash does not change the recipe signal. Deleted ingredient entries are ignored when the colors and category dots are generated.

#

Amounts are not encoded. The mark does not show quantities, weights, or recipe proportions. Open the batch record for exact ingredient amounts.

!

Check the batch, not just the mark. The emblem cannot tell you that fermentation is finished, that pressure is stable, or that a batch is safe to bottle. Use appropriate readings and your brewing process to make those decisions.
